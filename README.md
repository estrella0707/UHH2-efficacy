# Overall structure

## [Study 1: the phenomenon]
We want to test: If people feel more exposed to some risk, they also feel more inclined to blame someone, i.e. endorse a blame narrative. 
Treatment: Placebo (e.g., sunlit hour) vs Crime
DV: narrative endorsement
Manipulation checks on perceived control and affects

## [Study 2: basic mechanism]
We hypothesize the following Mechanism: When people face a negative information (compared to placebo), people experience a loss of control (see study 1); the compensatory control theory predicts that people will want to regain control. Control can come from various ways, so we provide participants with scapegoating and non-scapegoating explanations.
Treatment: 
1.baseline (3 crime slides)
2.baseline + scapegoating explanation of crime (drug addicts)
3.baseline + non-scapegoating explanation of crime
Compare the diffs between 1-2 and 1-3 – the regain of control is larger with the scapegoating explanation
DV: perceived control

## [Study 3: implication]
We want to test: This effect becomes the stronger the less people feel in control (LOW efficacy compared to HIGH efficacy) with regard to this risk.
Treatment: Baseline VS Low VS High
DV: narrative endorsement, memory as alternative mechanism to rule out
